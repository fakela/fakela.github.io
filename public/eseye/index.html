Eseye Documentation V2Most recent work · Eseye, IoT connectivity · docs.eseye.com · 2026

Eseye's documentation lived in MadCap Flare and a handful of other places, and only a specialist could change it. The brief was open-ended and the project spanned several brands, so the first job was turning that ambiguity into a clear plan. Over the last six months I moved all of it to GitBook and rebuilt how it's written, reviewed and published. Engineers, product and support staff can now suggest or make changes directly, AI assistants can answer from the content, and one source serves customers, internal staff and partner brands.

What I delivered

  • Chose the platform. Evaluated Mintlify, Document360, Fern and GitBook, built proofs of concept and recommended GitBook.
  • Moved everything off MadCap Flare. Migrated the full documentation set using a Python pipeline I wrote to convert pages, fix images and rebuild navigation. Every change now goes through Git sync and automated checks.
  • Launched public and internal sites. docs.eseye.com for customers, and an internal site staff sign in to with their Microsoft accounts.
  • Set up partner sites from one source. Shared content feeds separately branded sites for AT&T, MTN and TELUS, so a change is written once and reaches every partner.
  • Made the docs AI-ready. Used AI in drafting and review, and structured pages so the built-in AI assistant and external AI tools can find and quote accurate answers.
  • Opened the docs up to subject matter experts (SMEs). Wrote guides and templates for people who contribute through a simple template, through GitHub or directly in GitBook, then trained colleagues so they can publish changes themselves instead of waiting on a writer.

Same structure, different brands

Every partner site shares the page structure, content and card patterns. Only the logo, colours and illustrations change. Keeping that line clear is what lets a small team look after several sites.
